# ADR-002 — Thijs Institutional Website Strategy

**Status:** Proposed  
**Date:** 2026-06-16  
**Author:** OpenCode Agent  
**Related:** BFS-002 — Indian Institutional AI Worksheet Landscape; DFS-002 — Go-To-Market and Adaptive Loop Foundations

## Context

Thijs is packaging an AI tutor SaaS for Indian schools and tuition centres, starting with Natalmanjaya High School. The website must articulate the worksheet → OMR → adaptive loop, differentiate from existing edtech and OMR providers, and invite collaborators and early adopters. Prior research (BFS-002, DFS-002) confirmed:

- Institutions already buy point solutions (curriculum suites, adaptive apps, OMR scanners) yet lack an integrated workflow bridging printable worksheets and adaptive reassignment.[^1][^2]
- India’s education market is scaling toward US$313 billion by FY30 with edtech projected at ~US$30 billion by 2031, validating SaaS expansion opportunities.[^3]
- Principals, teachers, parents, privacy officers, and strategists require transparency around pedagogy, operations, data handling, and ROI.

The website must communicate this positioning while honouring the project’s human-values narrative and alumni-driven origin story.

## Decision

1. **Narrative architecture:** Adopt a homepage flow of (a) Hero value proposition, (b) Workflow explainer (worksheet → OMR → adaptive), (c) Outcomes proof from Natalmanjaya pilot, (d) Persona-specific benefits (teachers, principals, students/parents), (e) Pricing tiers for institutions, (f) Call-to-action for pilots and contributor recruitment.  
2. **Messaging pillars:** Lead with “AI tutor that handles the grind so teachers can teach,” emphasising human oversight, adaptive mastery, and alumni credibility.  
3. **Persona commitments:** Embed responses to the ten-perspective filter within content—efficiency, student dignity, parent visibility, compliance, SRE-readiness, ML transparency, curriculum alignment, open-source ethos, and business defensibility.  
4. **Content operations:** Publish research citations and ADR references on a dedicated “Why it Works” page; maintain change log aligning future product updates to the research-first covenant.  
5. **Design direction:** Plan a responsive layout with purposeful typography (non-default), warm academic palette, and contextual imagery (classrooms, worksheets, scanning). Avoid generic SaaS gradients; visualise the adaptive loop via annotated diagram.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Aligns website storytelling with validated market gaps, reinforcing strategic differentiation.  
- Provides stakeholders with transparent view of pedagogy, operations, and compliance posture.  
- Creates a reusable narrative template for future vertical pages (state boards, exam prep).

### Negative / Trade-offs

- Requires gathering pilot metrics and testimonials before launch; delays if data is not yet compiled.  
- Increased effort to maintain citations and ADR references could slow marketing iterations.  
- Persona-rich messaging may lengthen copy; needs careful editing to stay concise.

## Alternatives Considered

1. **Feature-first landing page** highlighting UI screenshots without research context — rejected because it fails the research-first covenant and overlooks institutional buyer concerns.  
2. **Student-only narrative** mirroring B2C exam-prep sites — rejected; revenue focus is institutional SaaS.  
3. **Minimal one-page teaser** pending product maturity — rejected; pilot launch demands clarity for procurement teams and collaborators.
