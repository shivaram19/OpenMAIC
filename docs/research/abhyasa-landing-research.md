# Abhyāsa Landing Page — Research Backing

## 1. Name rationale: why “Abhyāsa”

**Definition**
- *Abhyāsa* (अभ्यास) is the Sanskrit term for sustained, repeated practice or disciplined effort. It is the core method Patanjali prescribes for steadying the mind and building mastery [T1].
- In the *Yoga Sūtras*, Patañjali defines it as the continuous effort to attain steadiness of mind, and declares:
  > “Sa tu dīrghakāla-nairantarya-satkārādarāsevito dṛḍhabhūmiḥ”  
  > “That practice becomes firmly established when cultivated with devotion, without interruption, over a long time.”  
  > — Yoga Sūtra 1.14 [T1][T2].
- The same root appears in the *Bhagavad Gītā* where Krishna teaches abhyāsa as the essential means to control the mind, together with vairāgya (non-attachment) [T3].

**Why it fits the product**
- Worksheets are not tests; they are *repetitive, deliberate practice*. The name signals that the product helps students build mastery through consistent effort rather than last-minute cramming.
- For Indian parents and educators, the word carries positive cultural weight: discipline, perseverance, and gradual improvement.
- The subdomain will be **`abhyasa.trayini.ai`**.

## 2. Target audience and emotional landscape

### 2.1 Tuition-centre owners / budget-school principals
- **Pain points**: photocopying generic papers, spending hours creating differentiated worksheets, no visibility into which student is weak on which topic, tight budgets, high parent expectations.
- **Emotions**: anxiety about losing students to bigger coaching brands, pride in personal teaching quality, frustration with admin workload.
- **Decision drivers**: cost per student, time saved, board/exam alignment, ease of printing on existing hardware, data safety.

### 2.2 Teachers
- **Pain points**: mixed-ability classrooms, repetitive grading, lack of feedback loops, pressure to finish syllabus.
- **Emotions**: empathy for struggling students, burnout from admin, desire to see visible progress.
- **Decision drivers**: how fast they can generate a worksheet, whether it respects their board’s reduced syllabus, whether it reduces (not increases) workload.

### 2.3 Parents
- **Pain points**: unsure if child is practising the right topics, limited insight beyond marks, comparison with peers, exam fear.
- **Emotions**: hope for child’s success, worry about wasted tuition fees, desire for transparency.
- **Decision drivers**: personalised practice, progress reports, safety of child data, affordability.

### 2.4 Students
- **Pain points**: boring one-size-fits-all worksheets, repeated mistakes, exam pressure.
- **Emotions**: discouragement from generic difficulty, confidence when questions match their level.
- **Decision drivers**: questions feel doable, instant feedback, clear next steps.

### Evidence from the market
- Vedantu’s 2025 parent/student survey highlights: lack of personal attention, one-size-fits-all teaching, exam fear, limited feedback, and irregular schedules as the top exam-prep pain points [T4].
- A CBSE-focused exam guide identifies the “Preparation–Execution Gap”: students often misread questions, write beyond word limits, and leave questions blank because they have not practised under realistic conditions [T5].

## 3. Conversion and messaging principles

| Principle | Why it matters for Abhyāsa | Source |
|-----------|---------------------------|--------|
| **Single, repeated primary CTA** | Indian B2B2C buyers have low patience; one clear action reduces decision paralysis. | [T6] |
| **Value proposition above the fold** | Visitors decide within seconds; headline + subhead + CTA must answer “what is it, why me, what next.” | [T7] |
| **Benefit-led copy, not feature lists** | Owners care about time saved and results; parents care about confidence and marks. | [T6] |
| **Transparent pricing or clear tiers** | Hidden pricing creates distrust; tiers help self-qualification. | [T8] |
| **Trust badges and privacy signals** | Schools/parents are sensitive to child data; DPDP-ready, India-hosted, no-ads messaging lowers risk. | [T6][T9] |
| **Social proof / usage stats** | “Used by X centres” or short testimonials borrow credibility for an early-stage product. | [T9] |
| **Minimal, frictionless form** | Each extra field reduces submissions by 4–7%; ask only what is essential for a pilot. | [T9] |
| **Mobile-first, fast page** | Many owners and parents browse on phones; slow pages increase bounce. | [T7][T9] |
| **FAQ section** | Addresses objections (cost, data safety, board support) and improves AEO/SEO visibility. | [T6] |

## 4. Design and emotional tone

- **Primary color: deep teal (#0d7377)** — teal sits in the trust-color family but feels warmer than cold clinical blue; appropriate for education, healthcare, and high-stakes tools [T10].
- **Accent: amber (#f59e0b)** — used sparingly for CTAs and highlights; signals warmth and attention without the aggression of red [T11].
- **Neutrals: warm stone (#fafaf9, #f5f5f4, #e7e5e4, #78716c, #1c1917)** — warm greys feel approachable and trustworthy, avoiding the sterility of pure grey [T12][T13].
- **60-30-10 rule**: ~60% neutral surfaces, ~30% secondary text/borders, ~10% accent to keep CTAs powerful [T13].
- **Typography**: clean sans-serif (Geist/Inter), strong size contrast, readable line length.
- **Motion**: minimal; opacity/transform only, no ornamental animations, to respect users in heightened-vigilance states [T10].
